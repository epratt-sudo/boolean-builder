Boolean Search Builder

Turn your research question into search terms you can paste into the Advanced Search of any library database. Work through the four cards in order.

Example loaded. Look around, then clear it and type your own question.
Step 1

Write your research question

Write it as a full question. Be as specific as you can about who, what, and where.

Step 2

Pick the main keywords

Tap the words that carry the meaning of your question. Grey words are question words (how, does, affect) that databases don't need. If two picked words belong together, tap the + between them to make a phrase.

Step 3

Build a keyword bank

Authors don't all use the same words. Add synonyms and related terms for each keyword. Then use Truncate to search every ending of a word at once.

  • Formal vs. everyday: adolescents / teens
  • Specific examples: social media / TikTok
  • Spelling variants: behavior / behaviour
  • Related problems: sleep / insomnia
Step 4

Build your advanced search

Open the database's Advanced Search and put each concept in its own search box. OR joins the synonyms inside a box, which makes your results bigger. Leave the dropdown between boxes set to AND, which narrows results to sources that mention every concept.

Leave something out (optional)

Add words with NOT to remove off-topic results, like adults. They go in their own box with the dropdown set to NOT. Use it sparingly, since it can remove good sources too.

Symbol cheat sheet
teen*Truncation: finds teen, teens, teenager, teenagers. "sleep deprivation"Quotation marks search the exact phrase in that order. AND · OR · NOTType the operators in capital letters.

Most databases use these symbols. If something doesn't work, check the database's Help page.

Results not working?

Too many results

  • Add another concept with AND
  • Use a more specific keyword
  • Make a truncation root longer
  • Use the date or peer-reviewed filters

Too few results

  • Add more synonyms with OR
  • Truncate more words
  • Remove your least important concept
  • Take out NOT terms
